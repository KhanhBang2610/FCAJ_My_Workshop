## Recap Report: Agentic AI Build Week (AABW) Hackathon - Highlighted Projects

The Agentic AI Build Week (AABW) Hackathon has concluded with highly innovative projects, focusing on solving real-world business problems using the power of Agentic AI and AWS. Below is a summary of 4 outstanding projects that participated in the event.

---

### 1. Team 3KA: S.H.E.P.H.E.R.D (Human-flow Management and Risk Prediction)

**Core Content:**
The S.H.E.P.H.E.R.D project focuses on Smart Human-flow Evaluation, Prediction, Hazard Detection, Response, and Dispatch. By combining YOLO, ByteTrack, and Amazon SageMaker with Amazon Bedrock AgentCore, the system turns ordinary camera footage into clear operational metrics: measuring crowd density, estimating queue conditions, and predicting early signs of congestion.

**Lessons Learned:**
- Building an AI product requires more than just a good model; it demands a stable overall architecture from video streaming and inference latency to an intuitive dashboard for operators.
- "Small, finished work beats big, broken ideas" - Focus on a core feature and make it work smoothly within the 24-hour limit of the Hackathon.

---

### 2. One Team: KFC Bot Agent (Multi-channel AI Ordering Assistant)

**Core Content:**
This project solves the "lost orders" problem that occurs when customers have to switch apps. KFC Bot Agent allows users to order directly on Zalo or Messenger through natural conversation.
Rather than a conventional chatbot, it is a true Agent: capable of understanding intent, planning, looking up menu data, applying discount codes, and verifying orders against the real cart. The "Design Once | Deploy Everywhere" architecture makes it easy to add new channels via adapters.

**Lessons Learned:**
- Natural language processing in ordering is highly complex (slang, mid-conversation intent changes). An AI Agent needs not only to understand language but also to connect closely with "tools" and Business Rules.

---

### 3. Plan V: Solution Architect Professional Native App (AI Automated Architecture Design)

**Core Content:**
The application aims to automate the workload of Solution Architects. Customers simply upload requirements or chat in natural language, and the system automatically analyzes requirements, drafts high-level architecture options, generates Drawio diagrams using standard AWS icons, and provides directional AWS cost estimates.

**Lessons Learned:**
- AI has the potential to free professionals from repetitive tasks such as reading Business Requirement Documents (BRD/PRD) or drawing diagrams manually. This allows them to focus on strategic thinking and critically reviewing the first "draft" generated by AI.

---

### 4. Signal Scout: Early Corporate Strategy Change Detection Platform

**Core Content:**
The project is designed to analyze financial metrics, operational data, and corporate signals to detect early changes in strategies or risks (e.g., restructuring). The system connects "scattered signals" into a clear story, providing reports and risk alerts via an intuitive Dashboard. All AI conclusions are backed by gathered factual data.

**Lessons Learned:**
- Transparency is key. In Decision Support systems, every AI inference must be traceable and verifiable with clear data evidence. Maintaining human-controlled decision-making remains an essential factor.

---

## Conclusion

The AABW Hackathon was not just a 24-hour coding competition, but a clear demonstration of the shift towards the **Agentic AI** era. The participating teams proved that AI is no longer limited to generating text or images, but is now capable of executing complex workflows, calling APIs, and making highly reliable decisions based on real data.
